# V1 provenance addendum

Recorded 31 October 2026 · Rob Tyrie / Ironstone Advisory

Documentation prepared with OpenAI Codex

This is a dated clarification of work preceding September 2026. It does not
revise the original v1 specifications or add v2 features to them. The April
2025–September 2026 README banner describes this repository's historical period,
not the beginning of the idea.

## Earlier development

Rob reports beginning the idea in **2022** and making a first version in **2024**.
Conversation retrieval in October 2026 found related inquiry in June 2023 and a
practical "Frankfurt 'Bullshit Meter' Analysis" in September 2024. Explicit
"Bullshit Compass" naming was recovered from March 2025, before the April drafts.

The 2022 and first-version dates are Rob's recollections. The retrieved 2024
assistant output supports an early review experiment, not a verified software
release or the complete first-version artifact. Full transcripts and persistent
conversation links were not returned. These evidence classes remain distinct.

## Influences and original contributions

Harry Frankfurt's *On Bullshit*, Gordon Pennycook and colleagues' research on
pseudo-profound bullshit, and David Graeber's work were foundational influences.
They were starting points for Rob's own framework and measures.

Rob identifies the measure arising from his observations of Jordan Peterson's
tautologies in arguments and exposition as his original project contribution,
rather than a measure borrowed from those thinkers. The inherited README calls
the combined pattern "Peterson Weakness"; the archived terminology includes TCR
and PWI, with PWI later refined/renamed as ISI in [spec-original](spec-original).

Peterson was the observational reference, not the author or endorser of the
measure. Original contribution, a proposed operational definition and evidence
of effectiveness are different matters. The current method examines textual
patterns regardless of author identity.

Pennycook's reader-receptivity research does not validate an automated document
score. Graeber supplies organizational context; the work-structure hypothesis
about "bullshit knowledge objects" belongs to the project's synthesis. Neither
attribution justifies judgments about an author's character or job.
